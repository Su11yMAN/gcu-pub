Get Charged Up

Overview & Rules

How the Get Charged Up Lightning pick’em game works: scoring, making picks, and every command you can reply with.

A private game — nothing is sold

Get Charged Up is a private, non-commercial hockey pick’em game run by Stephen Sullivan, Sole Proprietor for friends and family. It is not a business offering. Nothing is sold and nothing is bought. There is no fee, no subscription, no prize, no payout, no wager, no advertising and no sponsorship. No goods or services are provided, and no commitment of any kind is made to any participant. Nobody stands to gain or lose anything of value by taking part.

Effective September 14, 2026 · Tampa Bay Lightning season

The game in one paragraph

Before every Tampa Bay Lightning game, you pick one Lightning skater. You earn points from what that skater does in that game. A leader board tracks everyone across the season. That is the whole game. There is nothing to win and nothing to lose — see the Terms & Conditions.

The game runs itself. It watches the NHL schedule, sends an invitation before each game, reads your reply, keeps track of who is injured, scores the game when it ends, and sends a recap. You only ever have to do one thing: reply with a player.

How scoring works

Points come from your picked skater’s performance in that one game. They stack — a player who scores the Lightning’s first goal and later wins the game collects all of it.

What your player doesPoints
Each goal scored+1 each
Scores the Lightning’s first goal of the game+1
Scores the game-winning goal (shootout included)+1
Scores three or more goals — a hat trick+1

Playoffs multiply the whole game

In the playoffs the entire game total is multiplied. The multipliers are not cumulative — each round has its own.

RoundMultiplier
First round2×
Second round3×
Conference finals4×
Stanley Cup Final5×

The two rules people ask about

  • Shootouts. A player who wins it in the shootout gets the +1 game-winner bonus but no goal credit — a shootout goal is not an individual goal.
  • No game winner in a loss. If the Lightning lose, nobody is awarded the game-winner point.

Two separate leader boards

Preseason is scored on its own ledger. The regular season and the playoffs share a second one. Preseason points never carry into the season standings.

How to make your picks

  1. An invitation arrives by email, text, or both — whichever you chose. It lists every eligible skater, forwards and defence, with the current injury and scratch status beside each one. Goalies cannot be picked.
  2. Reply with one player. Either works:
    • a jersey number — 86
    • a name — Kucherov. Close enough spelling is fine; if it is genuinely ambiguous you will be asked which one you meant.
    In the email invitation, every player name is also a link that opens a reply already filled in — one tap, then send.
  3. Keep the four-character code in the subject line. Every message carries one, like [GB6R]. That is how your reply is tied back to the right game even days later or from a different thread. Simply hitting Reply keeps it for you.
  4. Change your mind freely. Reply again with a different player and the new one replaces the old. The last pick before puck drop is the one that counts. After puck drop, picks are locked.
Only one game is ever open at a time

An invitation goes out up to three days before puck drop, and never before the previous game’s results have gone out. So there is never a question of which game you are picking for — there is only ever one.

Reminders

If you have not picked yet, the invitation is re-sent as a reminder one hour before the game’s start time of day, each day until the game. A 5:00 pm Sunday game nudges at 4:00 pm on Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Picking stops them immediately, and so does puck drop. Each reminder is freshly rendered, so it shows the current injury report rather than whatever was true when the first invitation went out.

Commands you can use in a reply

These work the same way by text or email. Send one on its own and it is recognised; capitalisation does not matter.

HELP What this program is and how to stop it.

WELCOME, HELP, STOP and START work at any time, even when no game is open. The rest need an open game, since they describe one.

Text and email are separate channels: STOP sent by text ends the texts and leaves the emails running. Full details are on the Voluntary Participation page.

Reading the game numbers

Every game carries a number after the team names, and its shape tells you which part of the season it belongs to.

SeasonLooks likeMeans
PreseasonLightning vs Predators (P1) Preseason game 1
Regular seasonLightning at Rangers (G01) Regular-season game 1
PlayoffsLightning at Canadiens (R1G3) Round 1, game 3

A postponement that reorders the calendar renumbers everything cleanly, so the numbers always read in true order.

Where the numbers come from

Schedules, rosters, scoring, shootouts, scratches, records and season statistics come from the NHL’s public feed; injury reports come from ESPN’s league-wide feed. Both are public sources we do not control, and both are occasionally late or wrong. Scores may be corrected after the fact if the source data changes. Any scoring question is settled informally by Stephen Sullivan, Sole Proprietor, and since nothing is at stake, settling it takes about four seconds.
